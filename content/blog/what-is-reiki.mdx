Reiki is a gentle, hands-on (or hands-near) healing practice that originated in Japan in the early twentieth century. The word itself comes from two Japanese characters — *rei*, meaning universal, and *ki*, meaning life energy — and the practice is built on a simple idea: that a calm, focused transfer of energy can support the body's own capacity to rest, repair and find balance.

## What actually happens in a session

A typical session is quiet and unhurried. You stay fully clothed, usually lying down or seated comfortably, while the practitioner places their hands lightly on or just above different areas of the body — head, shoulders, torso, limbs — pausing at each for several minutes. There's no massage, no manipulation, nothing that requires you to do anything at all. Most people simply lie still, breathe, and let their mind wander.

Many clients describe a sense of warmth or gentle tingling where the practitioner's hands rest, a heaviness in the limbs, or simply the kind of deep relaxation that's hard to reach on your own. It's common to drift into a light sleep. Afterwards, people often talk about feeling lighter, calmer, or like something they'd been carrying has eased.

## Why people come to reiki

There's no single reason. Some people come during a stretch of high stress or burnout, looking for a space where they don't have to perform or explain anything. Others come alongside other forms of care — physical or emotional — as something gentle to sit next to it. Some come simply because they tried it once and wanted to feel that calm again.

Reiki isn't a replacement for medical care, and a good practitioner will never suggest it is. What it offers is something adjacent: a slow, attentive hour where your only job is to receive.

## What to expect from your first session

If you've never had reiki before, the most useful thing to know is that there's very little to prepare. Wear something comfortable, arrive a few minutes early to settle in, and go in without expectations either way — everyone experiences it a little differently, and there's no "right" way to respond.

If you're curious, a taster session is a low-pressure way to find out what it feels like for you.
