What Is Reiki? A Beginner’s Guide to Reiki Healing
If you’ve ever heard of Reiki but wondered what it actually is, you’re not alone.
Reiki is an ancient art of healing and channeling. For some people, a Reiki session is a deeply relaxing experience; for others, it creates space to reflect and reconnect with themselves on an energetic level.
At Divine Align Healing, I use Reiki as part of a holistic approach to wellbeing, creating a calm space where you can slow down, turn your attention inward and simply take some time for yourself.


What does Reiki mean?
The word Reiki comes from the Japanese words ‘rei’, meaning universal or spiritual, and ‘ki’, meaning life-force energy.
The concept behind Reiki is that life-force energy flows through and around us. Reiki practitioners act as a channel for this energy, allowing it to flow through their hands during a treatment.
There are different traditions and ways of practising Reiki, but at the heart of a Reiki session is the intention to create a peaceful environment for relaxation and energetic wellbeing.


How does Reiki work?
During a Reiki treatment, I begin by taking a few moments to connect with the energy around me and set an intention for the session. I then call in Reiki energy from the universe before channelling it through my hands.
You may experience Reiki through gentle hands-on touch or with my hands held just above the body, depending on what feels appropriate for you.
You don’t need to do anything during the treatment. There is no need to concentrate, meditate or “clear your mind”. You can simply lie back, get comfortable and allow yourself to be present.


What happens during a Reiki session?
Your Reiki session begins before the energy work itself.
We start with a consultation, giving you the opportunity to talk about how you’re feeling and anything you’d particularly like to focus on during your appointment.
You might choose an intention for your session, such as:
* Feeling more grounded
* Creating a sense of calm
* Letting go of a stressful week
* Making time for yourself
* Reconnecting with yourself
* Simply allowing yourself to rest
You’ll then get comfortable on the treatment bed, fully clothed, and the Reiki treatment begins.
I work intuitively throughout the session, moving through different areas of the body and allowing the treatment to unfold naturally.
At the end, there is time to gently come back into the room and reflect on how you’re feeling. Depending on the session, you may also have the opportunity to choose an oracle card as a final point of reflection.
There is no right or wrong way to experience Reiki.


What does Reiki feel like?
Everyone experiences Reiki differently.
Some people describe feeling warmth, tingling, heaviness or lightness. Others notice a sense of deep relaxation or simply enjoy having uninterrupted time to rest.
You might experience very little physically and still find the session calming and restorative.
You don’t have to feel a particular sensation for Reiki to be a worthwhile experience. Every person’s experience is individual, and there is no expectation to “feel” anything.


Do you have to believe in Reiki?
No.
You don’t need to have a particular religious/spiritual belief system or understand energy healing before trying Reiki.
Some clients are very interested in spirituality and energy work, while others are simply curious or looking for a different way to relax.
You can approach a Reiki session with an open mind and simply see how the experience feels for you.


What are the benefits of Reiki?
People may choose Reiki because they want to:
* Relax and unwind
* Explore energy healing
* Feel more grounded and present
* Create space for reflection
* Develop a deeper connection with themselves
* Make their wellbeing more of a priority
* Receive a deeper sense of clarity 
It’s important that Reiki shouldn’t be used to diagnose, treat or cure medical conditions, and any health concerns should always be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional.


Is Reiki right for you?
You don’t need to know anything about chakras, crystals, energy or spirituality to enjoy a Reiki treatment.
If you’re curious, open-minded and would like to experience a different approach to relaxation and wellbeing, Reiki could be something worth exploring.
And if you’re already interested in energy healing, Reiki can be a beautiful way to deepen your connection with yourself and your energy.


Ready to experience Reiki for yourself?
At Divine Align Healing, my Reiki sessions are designed to give you a calm space to slow down, reconnect and turn your attention inward.
If you’ve never experienced Reiki before, you’re very welcome - I’ll talk you through everything before we begin, so you know exactly what to expect.

Reiki and the Meridians: Understanding Energy Pathways in the Body
When exploring energy healing, you may come across two terms quite frequently: Reiki and meridians.
Both are connected to traditional concepts of energy and wellbeing, but they come from different healing traditions and shouldn’t be treated as exactly the same system.
Reiki originated in Japan, while the meridian system forms an important part of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM). Understanding the distinction between them gives us a much more interesting insight into the different ways traditional healing systems have understood the relationship between energy and the body.
What is Reiki?
Reiki is a Japanese energy healing practice developed by Mikao Usui in the early 20th century.
The word Reiki is generally translated from Japanese as rei (often interpreted as universal or spiritual) and ki (referring to vital or life-force energy).
In the Reiki tradition, the practitioner acts as a channel for Reiki energy, allowing it to flow through their hands during a treatment.
During my own Reiki practice, I begin by creating a clear intention for the treatment and connecting with the Reiki energy before channelling it through my hands. I then work intuitively through different areas of the body, allowing the session to unfold rather than following a rigid sequence.


What are meridians?
Meridians come from a different tradition.
In Traditional Chinese Medicine, meridians are understood as pathways through which Qi (often translated as vital energy or life force) flows throughout the body.
There are 12 primary meridians, which are associated with particular organs and bodily functions. There are also additional meridian pathways, including the Du Mai (Governing Vessel) and Ren Mai (Conception Vessel).
The meridian system is used within several traditional practices, including acupuncture, acupressure, tuina and qigong.
Rather than thinking of meridians as literal tubes or physical structures that can be seen anatomically, they are best understood within the framework of Traditional Chinese Medicine as a traditional energetic map of the body.
This distinction is important because meridians aren’t equivalent to nerves, blood vessels or the lymphatic system.


The 12 primary meridians
The traditional meridian system describes 12 primary meridians, each associated with an organ system.
These are:
* Lung
* Large Intestine
* Stomach
* Spleen
* Heart
* Small Intestine
* Bladder
* Kidney
* Pericardium
* Triple Burner / San Jiao
* Gallbladder
* Liver
In TCM, these meridians form interconnected pathways rather than isolated systems.
They are also traditionally divided into Yin and Yang meridians, reflecting the complementary relationship between Yin and Yang within Chinese philosophy.


So, what does this have to do with Reiki?
This is where things become particularly interesting.
Although Reiki and the meridian system come from different traditions, both work within traditional concepts of energy flowing through and around the body.
However, it is important not to conflate the two.
Reiki does not traditionally involve diagnosing or manipulating individual meridians in the same way that acupuncture or acupressure does.
A Reiki practitioner isn’t necessarily working on the Liver Meridian, for example, simply because their hands are positioned over an area associated with it.
Instead, Reiki follows its own Japanese energetic framework.
The connection between the two is therefore more accurately described as an interesting point of comparison between different traditional energy systems, rather than Reiki being a form of meridian therapy.


Reiki, Qi and Ki: are they the same thing?
You may also notice the terms Ki and Qi and wonder whether they’re referring to exactly the same thing.
The Japanese word Ki and Chinese Qi share the same Chinese character and are related linguistically and philosophically. Both can broadly be translated as vital energy or life force.
However, the concepts have developed within their respective cultural and healing traditions.
Qi is central to Traditional Chinese Medicine and Chinese philosophy, while Ki forms part of Japanese traditions including Reiki.
So while there are similarities, it is more accurate to understand them within their own cultural contexts rather than simply saying that Qi and Ki are interchangeable.


The body’s energetic map
One of the things I find fascinating about energy work is the number of different traditional systems that have developed ways of understanding the body beyond its physical anatomy.
Reiki practitioners may work with concepts including:
* Energy flow
* Chakras
* Aura
* Intention
* Universal life-force energy
Traditional Chinese Medicine, meanwhile, works with concepts including:
* Qi
* Yin and Yang
* Meridians
* Five Elements
* Zang-Fu organ systems
These frameworks aren’t identical, but they each provide a traditional way of thinking about the relationship between the body, mind and energetic wellbeing.


What about the chakras?
You may be wondering where the chakras fit into all of this.
Chakras originate primarily from Indian spiritual and yogic traditions and are therefore another distinct energetic framework.
The seven-chakra system commonly discussed in modern Western wellness practices includes:
1. Root
2. Sacral
3. Solar Plexus
4. Heart
5. Throat
6. Third Eye
7. Crown
The chakra system, Reiki and the Chinese meridian system are therefore three different traditions and energetic frameworks.
They can be explored alongside one another in modern holistic practice, but they shouldn’t be presented as though they are simply different names for the same thing.
Understanding these differences is part of approaching energy work with respect for the traditions from which these practices originate.




Can Reiki affect the meridians?
From a traditional Reiki perspective, a practitioner may understand the treatment as supporting the person’s overall energetic balance rather than deliberately targeting specific meridians.
There is currently no established scientific evidence demonstrating that Reiki physically moves through TCM meridians in the way that traditional Chinese medicine describes Qi moving through those pathways.


Why I find the relationship between Reiki and meridians fascinating
For me, learning about different energy systems adds another layer of understanding to holistic practice.
Reiki doesn’t require me to diagnose an imbalance within a particular meridian, nor does it replace the methods used by trained practitioners of Traditional Chinese Medicine.
Instead, it gives me another perspective on the body as a whole — and reinforces something that sits at the heart of my approach to holistic wellbeing:


We are more than just the physical body.
Our physical experiences, emotions, thoughts, environment and sense of wellbeing can all influence how we experience ourselves.
Whether you approach Reiki from a spiritual perspective, a relaxation perspective, or simply with curiosity, it can provide an opportunity to pause, become more aware of yourself and the energetic imbalances your body may be experiencing 


Exploring energy work for yourself
You don’t need to understand every energetic system before experiencing Reiki. In fact, you don’t need to have any previous experience with energy healing at all!

The Moon & Your Energy: Why I Live by the Lunar Cycle
I’ve always been fascinated by the idea that we are part of something much bigger than ourselves.
The more I learn about holistic wellbeing, energy and the natural world, the more I find myself coming back to one thing: the Moon.
I see this as a beautiful framework for checking in with myself - a reminder that life isn’t supposed to be one constant state of doing, achieving and pushing forwards.
There are times for growth.
Times for action.
Times for reflection.
And times for letting go.
The Moon reflects that beautifully.


Why does the Moon fascinate me so much?
The Moon has been observed, followed and given meaning by cultures around the world for thousands of years.
For me, it brings a sense of perspective.
It reminds me that I’m part of nature rather than separate from it. And that is something I’m becoming increasingly interested in through my own holistic journey.


Understanding the lunar cycle
The Moon takes approximately 29.5 days to complete one cycle from New Moon to New Moon.
During this time, we see different amounts of the Moon’s illuminated surface from Earth.
The four main phases are:
🌑 New Moon
🌓 First Quarter
🌕 Full Moon
🌗 Third Quarter
Between these major phases, the Moon gradually waxes - becoming more illuminated - and then wanes - becoming less illuminated.
For me, these phases provide a natural rhythm that I can use to reflect on what’s happening in my own life.


🌑 New Moon: planting the seed
The New Moon is the beginning of the lunar cycle.
It’s when the Moon is positioned between the Earth and Sun, meaning the side illuminated by the Sun is facing away from us and the Moon is barely visible in the night sky.
Energetically and spiritually, the New Moon is often associated with new beginnings, intentions and planting seeds.
This is one of my favourite times to pause and ask myself:


What do I want to create?
Rather than setting a huge list of goals, I like thinking about how I actually want to feel and what I want to make more space for.


Maybe it’s more creativity.
More rest.
More confidence.
More connection.
Or simply more time for myself.


I find that setting an intention feels very different to putting pressure on myself to achieve something.
🌓 Waxing Moon: taking action
As the Moon begins to become more visible, it enters its waxing phase.
Traditionally, this period is associated with growth, building and taking action.
This is where I like to take the intentions I’ve set and start putting some energy behind them.
It’s a reminder that intentions are lovely, but they also need action.
If I’ve decided I want to prioritise my wellbeing, what am I actually going to do about it?
If I want to grow something in my life, where can I put my energy?
The waxing Moon reminds me to move forwards - but not necessarily to rush.


🌕 Full Moon: illumination & awareness
The Full Moon is perhaps the phase most people associate with lunar energy.
Scientifically, a Full Moon occurs when the Moon is positioned approximately opposite the Sun from Earth’s perspective, allowing us to see the Moon’s fully illuminated side.
Spiritually, Full Moons have traditionally been associated with illumination, awareness, completion and release.
This is a time when I like to reflect.


What’s been happening in my life?
What have I learned?
What has become clearer?
What am I proud of?
And perhaps most importantly:


What am I ready to let go of?
I don’t see “letting go” as necessarily meaning walking away from something dramatic.
Sometimes it’s letting go of an old thought pattern.


An expectation.
A habit.
A version of yourself that you’ve outgrown.
Or simply something you’ve been carrying that you don’t need to carry anymore.
🌗 Waning Moon: slowing down
After the Full Moon, the illuminated part of the Moon begins to decrease.
This is the waning phase.
And this is where I think the lunar cycle has an especially important message for modern life.
We’re constantly encouraged to do more.


Work harder.
Be more productive.
Achieve more.
Improve ourselves.


But nature doesn’t work like that.
The Moon doesn’t stay full.
Trees don’t stay in bloom.
The seasons change.
And we aren’t designed to be operating at maximum capacity all of the time either.
I see the waning Moon as an invitation to slow down, reflect and create space.
It’s a reminder that rest isn’t the opposite of growth.
Rest is part of the cycle.
🌑 Then we begin again
Eventually, the Moon reaches another New Moon and the cycle starts again.
And I love that.
Because it means you get another opportunity to begin. You don’t have to get everything right in one cycle. You can reflect on what worked.
Learn from what didn’t.
Release what no longer feels right.
And start again.
This is one of the reasons I find living with the lunar cycle so grounding.
It gives me permission to see life as something that moves in cycles rather than in a straight line.


Why I’m learning so much about the Moon right now
The more I learn about Reiki, energy and holistic wellbeing, the more curious I become about the wider traditions and practices surrounding them.
I’m still learning. And that’s something I actually really value.
I don’t want to present myself as someone who knows everything. The world of energy work, spirituality and holistic wellbeing is huge, and there is always another tradition, concept or perspective to explore.
At the moment, the lunar cycle is something I’m particularly drawn to.
I’m learning about the different phases, the symbolism associated with them, how different cultures have worked with lunar cycles and how I can incorporate some of these practices into my own life.
And the more I learn, the more I realise that living with the Moon isn’t about following a set of rules.
It’s about becoming more aware.


How I personally work with the Moon
I don’t have a strict ritual that I have to follow every single month. Instead, I use the different phases as gentle reminders.
Around the New Moon, I might journal about what I want to create or invite into my life.
During the waxing Moon, I might focus more on taking action.
Around the Full Moon, I like to reflect on what has become clear and what I’m ready to release.
During the waning Moon, I try to give myself more permission to slow down and turn inward.
Sometimes that might involve journaling.
Sometimes meditation.
Sometimes pulling an oracle card.
Sometimes getting outside and actually looking at the Moon.
And sometimes it’s simply knowing what phase we’re in and allowing that awareness to influence how I approach my week.


October’s Full Moon
As I’m writing this, we’re approaching the Full Moon on 26 October 2026.
For me, this makes the end of October a lovely opportunity to pause and reflect before moving into the next cycle.
Instead of asking yourself what you need to achieve next, try asking:


What am I ready to release?


What has been taking up space in your mind?
What are you holding onto because you’re afraid of what comes next?
What no longer feels aligned with who you’re becoming?
And what would you like to create space for?


You don’t need to have all the answers.
Sometimes simply asking the question is enough.


You don’t have to be spiritual to work with the Moon
This is probably the most important thing I’d say.
You don’t have to identify as spiritual.
You don’t need to believe in energy healing.
You don’t need crystals, candles, rituals or a collection of moon journals.
You can simply use the Moon as a reminder to slow down and check in with yourself.
For me, that’s what holistic wellbeing is ultimately about.
It’s not about doing everything perfectly.
It’s about becoming more aware of yourself — your body, your emotions, your energy, your needs and the way you move through the world.
And if the Moon gives me a reason to pause and reconnect with all of that, then I think that’s pretty special.


A little Full Moon reflection
If you’re reading this around the October Full Moon, take five minutes tonight.
Look up at the Moon if you can.
Take a few deep breaths.
Then ask yourself:
What am I grateful for?
What have I learned?
What am I ready to release?
What do I want to make space for next?
There doesn’t need to be a perfect answer.
Just give yourself the space to listen.
Because sometimes we already know what we need - we just don’t give ourselves enough quiet to hear it.
